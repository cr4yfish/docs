---
title: "People and teams"
description: "Invite people to your workspace, put them on teams, and let each team decide what its people may do and which data they work with."
---

**People are on teams. A team says what its people may do and what data they work with.** There are no roles to pick for a person any more: everyone in the workspace may do the same basic things, and their teams add the rest. Two teams are always there:

- **Everyone** — every person in the workspace. Nobody is added or removed by hand. Its page says what every person may do and holds the **shared data**.
- **Admins** — the people who may do everything, except deleting the workspace, and who see all data. One of them is the workspace's **owner**.

Both live under **Workspace settings → People and access**, which has two pages: **Teams** and **People**. Every member can open Teams. People opens for whoever may see or invite people.

<Tip>
  Using Microsoft Entra ID, Okta or another SCIM directory? Let it add and remove people for you — see [Provisioning](/workspaces/provisioning).
</Tip>

## Teams

**Workspace settings → Teams** lists **Everyone** and **Admins** at the top, then every team. Each row says how many people are on it (and whether you are), where its people come from — added here or from Microsoft 365 — what it may do on top of everyone (*Nothing extra*, *Plus billing* …) and its data (*Own data · seen by 2 more teams*, *Sees all data* …). Search finds a team by its name, by something it may do, by a class of its data or by a source.

A **Needs you** section appears when there is something to decide: new groups from your directory (**Make it a team** or **Not a team**), [data without a team](#data-without-a-team), or the team limit reached.

Every member sees every team and its name — team names are not secret. What you see inside a team follows your permissions: a section you may not read says who it is shown to instead.

### A team's page

A team opens on a page of its own, read top to bottom. Each section has one **Change** button that opens the [guided flow](#create-or-edit-a-team) at exactly that question.

| Section | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **May do** | What the team's people may do on top of what everyone may do. A line marked **Sees all data** makes them admins for the database |
| **Its data** | The team's own classes and records in the [knowledge model](/workspaces/overview#knowledge-model). A team can also keep no data of its own |
| **Its files** | The sources filed with the team's data, with who sees each |
| **Who sees its data** | Other teams that see the team's data, at **Can view** or **Can edit**, Admins, and whether everyone else sees it. **See all** lists every person, with the reason |
| **Who may change its model** | Teams that may change the data's classes, properties and processes — separate from seeing its records |
| **Also sees** | Other teams' data this team is shown, and the shared data |
| **People** | The first people on the team. **See all** opens them on the People page |
| **Managed by** | Who may add and remove the team's people |
| **Onboarding questions** | What the team asks people who join it — see [Team questions](/workspaces/setup-guide#team-questions) |
| **Starter kit** | Up to six **first questions**, shown under **Try asking** on a new chat's empty screen and on a new member's [Home](/workspaces/overview#home-jump-back-in-and-for-you). While [chat templates](/workspaces/overview#chat-templates) are switched off, the kit doesn't ask for templates; picks made before are kept |

**Everyone**'s page shows how many people are in the workspace, what every person may do and the shared data. **Admins**' page lists who the admins are and says they may do everything except delete the workspace.

### Create or edit a team

**New team** and **Edit team** open the same guided flow: one question a screen, with a progress bar. In edit mode the counter is a menu that jumps to any question, and **Save and close** is on every screen.

1. **What is the team called?**
2. **Who is on it?** People you pick, a group from Microsoft 365, or nobody yet.
3. **What may its people do, on top of what everyone may do?** Pick from groups such as Apps, Agents and tools, Knowledge, See governance, Billing or Workspace settings. A group with parts asks which parts on the next screen. Ticking something that makes people admins for the database warns you on that screen, with how many people it affects.
4. **Does it keep data of its own?**
5. **Who else sees its data?** Nobody else, some teams (each at **Can view** or **Can edit**), or everyone.
6. **Who may change its model?** Only admins, the team itself, or other teams.
7. **Which other teams' data does it see?**
8. **What should a new chat suggest to them first?** The starter kit.
9. **Review.** Every change as before → after, with who it affects, and one **Save**.

Nothing is saved before the last screen. Your answers are kept in the browser as a draft, so a reload or Back lands on the same question. When you save, Genie first applies everything that takes access away, then the team itself, then everything that gives — so a failed removal never ends with more access than before. If only part of a save lands, the last screen lists what is left and **Try again** sends only that. A line someone else changed in the meantime is reported, not overwritten.

You can never give what you may not do yourself, and nobody can put themselves on a team. A team your directory fills can't be given anything that makes its people admins for the database.

### Billing without admin rights

The **Billing** group — **See billing** and **Manage billing** — can be given to any team without making its people admins. Billing never decides what data someone may read, so a finance team gets the Billing page and nothing else an admin can do:

| Who | What the team may do | Sees all data |
|---|---|---|
| Controller | See billing | No |
| Whoever pays | See billing and Manage billing | No |
| IT admin | Admins | Yes |

**See billing** opens the [Billing page](/workspaces/overview#workspace-billing), its usage by app and person, and the credit tools for Genie AI and [MCP](/workspaces/mcp-api#workspace-management). **Manage billing** adds buying credits, changing the plan and the payment portal. Someone who sees billing but isn't an admin sees an app that isn't shared with them as *Deleted or not shared with you* beside its credits. A team your directory fills can hold billing too.

### Who manages a team's people

People who may manage teams change who is on any team. You can also let a team manage only some teams: when you tick **Manage teams and their people** in the flow, choose **Every team** or **Teams I pick**. A team that manages Sales, for example, can add and remove Sales's people — and nothing else. The team's **Managed by** section shows who that is.

### Data without a team

Data in the knowledge model that no team holds shows up on the Teams page for owners and admins: *2 sets of data belong to no team*. Open one to decide whose it is — an existing team's, a new team's, or everyone's. The last screen says which teams and how many people gain or lose it before anything moves. Large sets move in the background and can be paused, resumed or stopped. Until you decide, the data is seen exactly as before.

### Delete a team

**⋯ → Delete** on a team's page asks what happens to the team's data before the team goes: hand it to another team or to everyone. Deleting a team never deletes data, and the confirmation says how many people lose what the team let them do. Files kept in access areas from before teams are listed under **Files from before teams** on the Teams page.

### Teams that were roles

Custom roles from before teams are listed under **Teams that were roles**, tagged *Was a role*. Their people keep what they had — nothing is converted behind anyone's back. Such a team has May do and People, but no data or starter kit. Press **Make it a full team** to give it those; the confirmation says what changes first, including that its people then may do at least what everyone may do. New roles can't be created any more: a new set of things people may do is a new team.

### Your home team

**Your home team** is the one of your teams you start from: it picks the starter kit a new chat opens with. Choose it on your own page under **People**. It opens nothing by itself.

When you share an item, prefer a team over naming people one by one — whoever joins the team later gets the same. A [directory group](/workspaces/provisioning#roles-and-teams-from-groups) can keep a team's people in step with your identity provider. Genie AI can read teams, change who is on one and what it may do, hand over data without a team, and set your home team — every change through a [confirmation card](/workspaces/overview#confirmation-cards).

## People

**Workspace settings → People** lists everyone in the workspace, 50 at a time, with search. Each row names the person's teams. Open a team's **See all** to see the list filtered to that team, with a chip that removes the filter. At the top, a line says where people come from — your company directory or invites here — with **Manage** for [directory sync](/workspaces/provisioning); **Rules** and **Invite** sit beside the title. What needs you is listed there too: an owner or admin your directory asked to remove, and people waiting for a free seat.

Someone who may only invite sees the invitations, not the list of people.

### A person's page

A person opens on a page of their own. Everyone can open their own page.

| Section | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Teams** | Each team they are on, and where that comes from — *From Microsoft 365* or *Added here by …* with the date. **Add to a team** and **Remove** where you may. On your own page, your **home team** |
| **May do** | What everyone may do, then one line per team that adds something, *through Team …*. Read-only: it is changed on the team |
| **Works with** | The data of their teams, what those teams also see, and the shared data, each with its level. Read-only |
| **Exceptions** | Things shared with this person alone — one class, property, relation, process or source. A row opens that item's Share dialog |
| **From before** | What the pages no longer offer, such as an old Editor assignment (*May do less than everyone*) with **Lift** |

A person never gets permissions of their own or a team's whole data: they get those through teams. Only small exceptions name a person. A person's own synced mail and drive stay private and are not on these pages.

### Remove someone

Open the person and choose **Remove** from the menu. They lose access to the workspace and all its projects immediately.

<Warning>
Removing someone is immediate and cannot be undone from the UI. To restore access, invite them again.
</Warning>

While the rule [The directory decides people and teams](#rules-for-the-whole-workspace) is on, people are removed in your company directory instead.

### Leave a workspace

Unless you are the workspace's owner of record, you can leave a workspace yourself from your own page under **People**.

<Warning>
If you are the workspace's owner of record, you cannot leave until you transfer ownership to someone else.
</Warning>

### Transfer ownership

The workspace's owner of record can hand it on. Open the person's page and press **Transfer ownership**, or ask Genie AI — it shows a card naming the person it found, and you confirm it there.

- **The new owner** becomes the workspace's owner.
- **You** keep your teams and can then leave or be removed like anyone else.
- **Billing does not move.** The subscription and payment details stay as they are until someone changes them under Billing; the transfer tells you so.
- **The owner of record can't be removed** — from the web app, Genie AI or the [MCP API](/workspaces/mcp-api) — until they've handed the workspace on.

## Invite people to your workspace

Open **Workspace settings → People** and press **Invite**. **An invitation gives what everyone may do — nothing else.** Put people on teams after they join: their teams decide the rest. The dialog offers two ways to invite:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="By email">
    Enter one or several email addresses — separated by commas, semicolons or new lines — and press **Send invitations**.

    - Each address gets its own personal invite and its own email. The invite works for 7 days, and only for that address.
    - Afterwards the dialog says per address how it went: sent, already a member (nothing is sent), or made but not delivered — then copy that invite's link and send it yourself.
    - One send takes a limited number of addresses; the dialog says how many. Each person can also send only a limited number of invite emails per day. Past that, share a link instead.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="By link">
    Create a shareable link and send it through any channel:

    - **Max uses** — how many people can use the link before it expires. Default is 1.
    - **Valid for** — how many days the link stays active. Default is 7.

    When someone opens the link and clicks **Join Workspace**, they are added immediately.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Every invite that still works — emailed ones included — is listed under **Invite links** until it is accepted or expires. Invitations sent before teams replaced roles still work as they were sent.

<Tip>
Create a link with a high **Max uses** count for open team onboarding (for example, when sharing a workspace link in a company-wide Slack channel), and invite by email for specific people.
</Tip>

<Note>
While the rule [The directory decides people and teams](#rules-for-the-whole-workspace) is on, nobody is invited here: the People page hides **Invite**, and people are added in your company directory.
</Note>

New people are offered a short [setup guide](/workspaces/setup-guide) — a chat with Genie AI — that tells them which teams they are on and asks their teams' onboarding questions.

## Set up access

A guided setup gets a new workspace's people, teams and access right in one go. Owners and admins who may manage members and document access start it from the top of the Teams page, or from the **People and teams** step of the [setup guide](/workspaces/setup-guide), which links to it. It offers two paths:

- **Company directory** — people, your verified domain, the directory connection, teams, what each team reaches, review.
- **By hand** — people, invites, teams, what each team reaches, review.

The setup saves as you go and has no Save button: close it and pick it up later, on another device, or let another admin finish it. Domains, teams, who is on them and invites take effect right away. What each team and everyone may reach stays a draft until you press **Start**: Genie suggests **Edit**, and *Decide later* changes nothing. **Review** lists who would lose access first, then who gains and what stays, and lets you see the workspace as one person would. **Start** applies exactly what Review showed. If someone else is already in the setup, you see who and can **Take over**. Genie AI can walk you through the same setup and starts it only through a [confirmation card](/workspaces/overview#confirmation-cards).

**With a directory, nothing changes until you turn on.** When you connect a company directory during the setup, it starts sending people and groups right away so they can be counted — but nobody joins, gets a team or is removed until you press **Turn on**. Before that, the review says how many people get in, how many removals from your directory are made, which groups fill their teams, which members your directory could not match (they stay), and whether your seats are enough. Turning on runs in the background with a progress bar; you can leave the page, and **Continue** picks it up if it stopped part-way. If access is never turned on, what the directory sent is deleted again after 30 days. A directory that was already running before the setup is not held — it keeps working as before.

## Rules for the whole workspace

**Rules** on the People page has two switches for the whole workspace. Only the workspace's owner can change them; each asks first and shows who is affected.

- **The directory decides people and teams.** While it is on, nobody is invited or removed here, no team is made here, and nobody changes who is on a team — your company directory does all of that. What a team may do, its data and its starter kit stay editable. Turning it on removes the members who were added here and are not in your directory; the confirmation names them first. Owners and admins stay, and teams made here stay, locked. The removed people's projects go to the workspace owner, and turning the rule off later does not bring anyone back. You can still leave a workspace yourself. The rule needs a connected directory.
- **Share with teams only.** While it is on, share lists name teams and everyone, never a single person — in every Share dialog, on a project's member list and for Genie AI. Lines that already name a person stay until someone removes them.

Genie AI can read both rules and changes one only through a [confirmation card](/workspaces/overview#confirmation-cards). MCP clients can [read them](/workspaces/mcp-api#workspace-management).

## Working in the same project at once

A workspace project supports one active editor at a time. If you open a project a teammate is already working in, you enter **spectator mode**: you can watch the chat and live preview update in real time, but the prompt input is disabled. A banner above the input shows who currently holds the project.

<Tip>
  You don't need to ask anyone to "give it up" — the lock clears automatically shortly after the other person stops actively working, and you can start typing as soon as it's free.
</Tip>
